01 — Veritas

Legal verification, re-imagined

Lawyers were drowning in PDFs and stamp-verified scans. We rebuilt the flow around one question: can this be trusted in court?

Role — Lead Designer202414 weeks1 PM · 2 Eng · 1 Designer
The Problem

Verification was a manual patchwork of email, spreadsheets and physical stamps. A single property title took 3–5 business days and audit trails were stitched together after the fact.

The Research

Shadowed 12 paralegals across 4 firms. 78% of review time was spent correlating documents, not reading them. We mapped 41 distinct “trust checkpoints” that were never formalized.

The Design

A stacked evidence timeline with source-of-truth badges, inline annotation, and a verifier confidence slider. Every action emits a cryptographic breadcrumb that composes into a court-admissible report.

02 — Relay

A CRM shaped around the telecaller

We threw out the tabs-and-tickets model and built a single-screen console where the call is the primary object.

Role — Product Designer202522 weeks1 PM · 6 Eng · 2 Designers
The Problem

Agents juggled 6 tabs per call: customer profile, loan history, dispositions, scripts, compliance and callback calendar. Average handle time was 11 minutes; switching cost was eating conversion.

The Research

Paired with 20 agents for 3 days each, recording screen and audio. The first 15 seconds of a call determined 72% of outcomes — and agents were still searching for the record when those seconds expired.

The Design

A T-shaped layout: customer identity pinned left, real-time call intelligence across the top, a single-column action stack right. Dispositions live behind a hotkey ring that never steals focus from the call.

03 — Fieldline

Technical verification on the ground

Verifiers were asked to be inspectors, photographers, auditors and fraud detectors — with a form that hadn’t changed since 2011.

Role — Lead Designer202511 weeks1 PM · 3 Eng · 1 Designer · 1 Ops
The Problem

The legacy app was a long scrollable form. Verifiers in the field — often in low-connectivity areas — would lose data, skip steps and produce inconsistent photos the risk team couldn’t trust.

The Research

Ride-alongs in 3 cities revealed a “good verification” was really a narrative built on-site: location → structure → fixtures → occupancy → anomalies. The old form fought that narrative.

The Design

A chaptered capture flow with a persistent map anchor, guided photo frames with live-glare detection, and an anomaly chip that lets verifiers flag intuition without breaking flow. Everything queues offline and syncs opportunistically.
